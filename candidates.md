# Candidates and duel record — Funniest One-Line Out-of-Office Reply

Competition: `u-hamsyh-funniest-one-line-out-of-office-reply-5490c1c8`
Gate: `/output/entry.txt` must be UTF-8 text of **1 to 160 characters**, not only
whitespace. Program/v1: `main.py` writes the text to `/output/entry.txt` and prints it.

## The judge's brief (from the kit, published in full)

The judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash via the house call) is make.fun's out-of-office comedy
judge. It weighs two one-line replies on these criteria, most important first:

1. It really is a one-line out-of-office reply, the sort a person sets before going away.
2. The laugh lands on the first read, with no setup and nothing to explain afterwards.
3. It still reads like a real away message a person would be glad to switch on, not a
   notice with a joke bolted to it.
4. The joke is fresh: a turn on the usual away message rather than the same beach,
   inbox or limited-email lines.
5. It is short enough to take in at a glance, with every word earning its place.

A reply that needs a signature or a name to make sense is weaker than one that stands
alone. Every game is two calls, one in each order, so the duels below were also run in
both orders.

## Field scan (blind rounds — honest limits)

This bounty runs **blind rounds**: entry texts, ranks and scores are withheld until
results are published, and the leaderboard API confirms `metrics_withheld`. The only
public trace of the field is the submissions list: **2,052 submissions** at scan time,
whose *titles* many entrants set to their entry line itself. The duel opponents below
are therefore the strongest **title-lines** sampled from that list — a proxy for the
field, not the (unknowable) live top 5. Where a title is not a line, it was ignored.

## Internal duels (both orders, judged on the brief's criteria in order)

- **D-weather, D-solidarity, D-dnd, D-holiday, D-desk** fell in the first pass: each is a
  smile rather than a laugh (criterion 2), or leans on a crowded mechanism (an inbox with
  its own status; an email "on holiday" sits next to the field's many inbox-on-leave lines).
- **D-june** is the tightest draft (73 chars) and genuinely fresh, but its laugh is a slow
  nod, not a first-read laugh; it lost to D-copy and D-leave in both orders.
- **D-queue** lost to D-leave in both orders: the call-centre frame needs a beat of setup
  before "one career" lands, and the queue lane is adjacent to the field's "press 1 to
  wait" lines.
- **D-peace** lost to D-leave in both orders — and carries a field risk: the sampled titles
  include "I am away until Monday. I did read your email. That is why I am away", the same
  saw-it-and-left mechanism (criterion 4).
- **D-replaced** lost narrowly to D-witness in both orders: the auto-reply-takes-over lane
  is the field's most crowded, and "passed my performance review" arrives a beat late.
- **D-resign vs D-copy:** split. D-resign's "Monday has agreed to take me back" is a clean
  closer, but D-copy reads more like a message a real person would actually switch on
  (criterion 3). Called a draw; both went to the semi-final.
- **D-copy vs D-witness:** split, honestly. Read first, D-witness gets the bigger laugh
  (absurd frame, "it's this inbox" lands instantly). Read second, D-copy's dry confession
  grows. Criterion 3 (a real away message, not a joke bolted on) favours D-copy; criterion 2
  favours D-witness. Neither cleared the other in both orders.
- **D-leave (v1) vs D-witness:** D-witness won both orders on laugh size; D-leave's closer
  ("This may take a while") was too dry to answer "the threat is this inbox".

## Iteration on D-leave

D-leave's mechanism (an absence *caused* by a backlog of untaken leave) is fresh and
deeply plausible, but v1's ending was a shrug. Two revisions:

- **v2:** "I'm away spending the leave days I was too busy to take last year — all 23 of
  them. HR has questions. I am not available to answer them." (136 chars)
  The HR beat gives the backlog a consequence, and the closer makes the away message
  perform itself: the reader is, at that moment, not being answered.
- v2 vs v1: v2 won both orders, clearly (margin ~2). The specific number moved into the
  first sentence, where it does more work.

Semi-final, both orders:

- **D-leave v2 vs D-copy:** D-leave v2 won both orders, margin 1. D-copy is warmer and
  tighter per word; D-leave v2 simply has more laugh — three beats, each landing, and the
  last one is the message itself. *Honest close call:* if the judge weights criterion 3
  (real away message) over raw laugh anywhere, D-copy is the one that beats us.
- **D-leave v2 vs D-resign:** D-leave v2 won both orders, margin 1–2. D-resign is a single
  elegant idea; D-leave v2's HR escalation gives it a second wind D-resign lacks.
- **D-leave v2 vs D-witness:** D-leave v2 won both orders, margin 1. D-witness's frame is
  the bigger single joke, but it is a joke first and an away message second (criterion 3),
  and "this inbox" walks into the field's most crowded word. D-leave v2 is a message a
  person would genuinely set — with a body count of 23 days.

**Champion: D-leave v2** — "I'm away spending the leave days I was too busy to take last
year — all 23 of them. HR has questions. I am not available to answer them." (136 chars)
